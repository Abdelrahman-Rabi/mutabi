# SMB impact: evidence and claims

This memo supports the Mutabi hackathon impact deck. Business outcomes are unmeasured; external surveys provide context, and the calculator supplies conditional arithmetic.

## Primary source selected for the deck

[Atradius Payment Practices Barometer: United Arab Emirates 2025](https://group.atradius.com/dam/jcr:ea4cb9db-19de-4ea4-8a78-db90458274e3/payment-practices-barometer-asia-2025-uae-en.pdf), printed pages 11 and 13, accessed 10 October 2026.

The FMCG section reports late payments affecting 56% of B2B invoices. The survey included 80 FMCG respondents within a UAE sample of 240 accounts-receivable contacts, across company sizes. Internet-panel interviews used quotas and ran in the second half of Q2 2025. This is UAE sector context, not an Egyptian SME estimate, an observed Mutabi baseline, or evidence that Mutabi reduces late payments. The source does not establish invoice recovery attributable to follow-up software.

Other primary research checked: [UK Department for Business and Trade, late payments impact research, 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/late-payments-research-impact-on-the-uk-economy) and [Intuit QuickBooks US Small Business Late Payments Report 2025](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/r/small-business-data/small-business-late-payments-report-2025/). These are different populations; their figures are not used as local baselines or calculator inputs. No suitably matched Egyptian distributor follow-up time benchmark was established in this research.

## Defensible business story

| Intended outcome | Product mechanism shown in synthetic cases | Evidence still needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staff capacity | Record commitments and prepare owned next actions | Matched operator time to a correct endpoint, including review, corrections and exceptions |
| Cash-flow visibility | Track due checks; route missed promises and unverified claims | Actual check completion and verified payment timing; longer comparison for collection/DSO effects |
| Reliable customer contact | Fresh balances and employee approval | Wrong/repeated reminders, accounting errors and operator/customer feedback |

Prototype tests establish behavior on tested cases. They do not establish customer savings, general model accuracy, collection uplift or market demand. A shadow pilot demonstrates workflow fit and operational measures; it cannot prove accelerated cash receipts when customer contact is not changed.

## Calculator scenario and break-even

All inputs remain illustrative: 1,000 monthly team tasks; 60% adoption; 8 manual vs 3 assisted minutes including review; SAR 50 loaded hourly staff cost; SAR 500 monthly recurring cost; SAR 2,000 setup.

- Adopted tasks: 600/month. Five minutes saved on each gives 50 hours/month.
- Released capacity is valued at SAR 2,500/month. Reassigned salaried time alone is not a cash saving.
- At 0% cash realization: cash benefit SAR 0, monthly net −SAR 500, no cash payback.
- At an assumed 40% realization: cash benefit SAR 1,000, monthly net SAR 500, setup payback four months.
- At 40% realization, recurring cash break-even requires 2.5 minutes saved per adopted task: 500 × 60 / (1,000 × 0.6 × 50 × 0.4). This threshold excludes setup recovery. Exactly at the threshold monthly net is zero, so setup does not pay back.
- At 0% realization no time saving covers positive recurring cash costs.

Capacity value and cash benefit overlap; do not add them. Cash realization must correspond to actual avoidable spending, such as reduced overtime or contractor payments. Costs are assumptions, not Mutabi pricing. The calculator excludes collection uplift, financing benefits and new revenue. Currency labels do not convert amounts.

## Pilot protocol

Recruit a distributor partner and one collections operator. Agree what counts as one task and a correct next action. Compare distinct equivalent manual/assisted cases; counterbalance order; log active staff time, elapsed time, review/correction time, correctness and facilitator help. Cover changed dates, installments, missed promises and unverified claims. Do not hide failed cases or trade correctness for speed. Report the operator and sample scope.

Then observe actual use, due-check completion, incorrect/repeated reminders and cash spending in a limited shadow pilot. Replace calculator assumptions with observed task time, adoption and avoidable spending. A longer accounting-linked study with a suitable comparison must separately assess collection timing, payment terms and sales mix. Earlier collection is liquidity, not new revenue.

## Suggested impact narration

“For a small distributor, follow-up is cash-flow work. Regional FMCG research illustrates the stakes. Mutabi makes the commitment, owner, next check and accounting evidence visible. If we save five minutes on 600 adopted tasks, that frees 50 hours a month. Whether that reduces spending depends on the business; our calculator makes the assumption visible. A pilot will replace these inputs with measured evidence.”

The submission asks for measured business impact. A transparent model strengthens the business case but does not satisfy that evidence requirement by itself. Actual matched operator measurements remain the most valuable next evidence.
